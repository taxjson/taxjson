taxjson.com1

The problem

The slip your broker files is not your cost basis

A T5008 reports proceeds per account. The Income Tax Act taxes you on one average cost per security across all your accounts, denies losses when any affiliated account — your RRSP included — bought the same thing within 30 days, and folds option premiums, splits, spin-offs and return of capital into that cost. Nobody's statement does that math for you.

Cross-account pooling

s.47: one ACB pool per identical property. A US listing and its TSX listing are the same pool; a CDR is not.

Superficial losses

s.54 / s.40(2)(g): a loss is denied if you or an affiliated person bought within ±30 days and still holds at day 30 — sized sale by sale. Denied into an RRSP, it is gone for good.

Everything that moves cost

Assignments (IT-479R), warrant exercises, splits, renames, spin-offs (s.86.1), mergers with elections, ROC, reinvested distributions — in the right order, on the settlement date.

Spreadsheets get the easy 90% right. The other 10% is where reassessments come from.

What it does

Three steps, all on disk

inputs/Broker CSV exports per account: IBKR, Questrade, RBC, Webull, Kraken, Coinbase — plus plain-text .tt for anything else
→
tjs runParse · convert at the Bank of Canada rate per settlement date · pool identical securities · dated renames and corporate actions with elections · superficial-loss pass across taxable and registered accounts
→
reports/Per-account summaries, Schedule 3 / Form 8949 rows, the wash radar, T1135, a tax and minimum-tax estimate — and the broker cross-check

Two project files

taxjson.toml: year, country, accounts (taxable / sheltered) and the figures you enter by hand. ticker.map: dated renames and symbol lookups. tjs migrate converts an older project.

Row accounting

Every parser accounts for every row it reads. Rows it recognises but does not translate are counted and named — never silently dropped.

Traceable numbers

taxjson audit recomputes every disposition from the parsed broker row through FX, ACB and the wash determination, and ties it out against the reports.

Worked example

A loss you cannot claim

What happened

The margin account sells 400 shares at a loss on February 3. On February 10 the RRSP buys 200 of the same stock, and still holds them on March 5.

What the slip says

A capital loss on 400 shares. The spreadsheet agrees.

What the Act says

CRA's formula: the least of 400 sold, 200 acquired in the 61-day window and 200 still held at its end — the loss on 200 shares is superficial (s.54). The ACB bump goes to the RRSP, where it is worthless: permanently denied. Shares the RRSP held before the window would not have counted.

NOTE: superficial loss — 200 sh denied (permanently: replacement property held in rrsp)
  loss sale 2026-02-03 · trigger lot 2026-02-10 (rrsp) · window end 2026-03-05
Only a tool that sees every account at once can get this right. That is the whole design.
taxjson.com5

Verification

Checked against the one thing that cannot lie

Internal reports can all agree and still be wrong. taxjson sanity compares the positions the books say you hold with the positions your broker's own export says you hold, account by account. Any difference is a trade that has not reached the CSV yet, or a booking problem — there is no third kind.

6,500+ tests

Every fix lands with a regression test that failed before it, tagged with the tax-logic rule it pins; the gate fails on a rule with no test.

Fuzzers and mutants

Three property fuzzers run thousands of random ledgers per run; mutation testing of both engines finds the boundaries no test pins.

Two full audits

About 1,300 and then 1,600 findings, after eight review rounds and a security audit. Every confirmed finding reproduced, fixed, pinned.

Published limits

KNOWN_ISSUES.md lists what is out of scope and what to do by hand, with the citation.

Privacy and security

Nothing personal leaves the machine

Local by construction

Parsing, pooling and the wash pass run on your computer — no server, no web UI, just commands and files. The pipeline's only network calls are FX rates (Bank of Canada, its noon rate before March 2017; Yahoo only before May 2007) and crypto prices for unpriced rows — both switchable off with TAXJSON_OFFLINE=1. SECURITY.md lists every egress.

No credentials in the core

The core holds no broker client and reads no credential. Broker fetch is a separate package (taxjson-fetch, installed by default; --without-fetch leaves it out): it uses your own token file, never writes it into a project, and sends it nowhere but that broker.

The project guards itself

A personal-data scan runs in every gate and as the pre-push hook, fails closed, and reads a private denylist kept outside the repository; the hook also refuses a commit message that quotes a money total. redact keeps every row shape and drops the identifiers it recognises — review before sharing.

Zero runtime dependencies in the core. A tax tool has to be trustworthy before it is clever.

Scope

Canada is the product. The US engine is a draft.

Canada — supported

Average-cost ACB, the superficial-loss rule across accounts, settlement-date timing, options and warrants, dated renames, corporate actions with elections, T1135, Schedule 3, a marginal-rate estimate with provincial tables, minimum tax line by line, instalments, and carry-forwards from one year's lock to the next. Validated against two years of real multi-account books and the brokers' positions.

US — experimental

FIFO, §1091 wash sales across your accounts, lots carried between your own accounts, §355 / §356 per lot, short/long-term, Form 8949 codes and a TXF export. Implemented and unit-tested, never validated on a real account. It says so at every run. Real US exports — redacted — are the way to change that.

The two rule sets never mix: a project has one country, and a setting, flag or command the other country owns is refused, not silently applied.

Honest labelling costs nothing. A tax tool that overclaims loses the only thing it has.

Install

Your first books in ten minutes

bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://taxjson.com/install.sh)"

Installs the latest release into its own Python environment and links taxjson and its short name tjs onto your PATH. Needs git and Python 3.9+, nothing else. Re-run to upgrade. The Questrade / IBKR auto-fetch plugin comes with it (add _ --without-fetch to leave it out).

mkdir -p ~/taxes/2026 && cd ~/taxes/2026
tjs init --country canada     # taxjson.toml, ticker.map, inputs/
# drop broker CSVs into inputs/margin/, inputs/rrsp/, …
tjs run                       # books, reports, wash pass, broker check
tjs sum                       # the year, by Schedule 3 line
tjs wash-radar                # "can I sell this at a loss today?"
tjs checklist                 # at year end: ready to file?
